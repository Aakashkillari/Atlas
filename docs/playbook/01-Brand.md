# 01 — Brand

> ATLAS Brand Constitution · Version 1.0

## Purpose

ATLAS exists to restore trust in internship allocation. It is not another internship platform; it is public infrastructure. The brand must reinforce trust, fairness, transparency, opportunity, and simplicity in every product decision.

## Identity

**Product name:** ATLAS — AI-driven Talent Linking & Allocation System
**Tagline:** *The Weight of Talent, Rightly Placed.*

## Principles

ATLAS feels professional, intelligent, calm, trustworthy, hopeful, human, national, and modern. It must never feel loud, needlessly futuristic, neon, gamer-like, crypto-inspired, hacker-themed, cyberpunk, or dependent on startup rainbow gradients.

The intended quality is government credibility with Apple craftsmanship, Stripe clarity, Linear simplicity, and Google usability. Usability is never sacrificed for visual effects.

When someone opens ATLAS, they should feel safe, supported, guided, optimistic, and empowered—never overwhelmed. See [01.5 Design Psychology](01.5-Design-Psychology.md).

## Rules

- Use “allocation”, “review”, “eligibility”, and “decision support” accurately; never promise a “guaranteed match” or declare a “best candidate.”
- Use India’s national identity respectfully. The flag inspires principles; it is not decoration or a repeating visual motif.
- Do not use dark patterns, urgency pressure, celebratory ranking imagery, unverified claims, or decorative AI effects.
- Icons assist recognition but never replace labels. Use one outline icon library consistently.
- Never create duplicate component variants without a documented need; see [02 Design System](02-Design-System.md).

## Standards

### Visual language

Neutral surfaces dominate. Use color intentionally: approximately 80% neutral surfaces, 15% blue information hierarchy, and 5% accent colors. Deep saffron communicates purpose and primary action; Ashoka Chakra navy communicates trust, authority, navigation, and information; green communicates success, growth, eligibility, and positive outcomes; red is reserved for danger.

Typography is the primary design element. Use readable, non-decorative type; high contrast; comfortable paragraph widths; and legible forms and tables. Whitespace is a feature: spacing communicates quality and prevents crowding.

Every layout answers: where am I, what can I do, and what should I do next. Navigation should not require thought. The product is mobile-first and must work on phone, tablet, laptop, and desktop.

### Product and landing experiences

The landing experience is emotional and tells the vision through restrained storytelling, trust-building visuals, statistics, and at most one meaningful premium 3D moment. After login, the product becomes fast, calm, efficient, minimal, and professional. See [12 Demo Strategy](12-Demo-Strategy.md).

## Examples

Use a verified-company badge with clear explanatory text and a transparent status indicator. Avoid generic corporate handshake photography, unlabeled icons, animated score reveals, and a dashboard styled like a gaming interface.

## Best practices

Use minimal, flat, purposeful illustrations only where they improve understanding. Images should represent students, learning, industry, technology, and opportunity. Apply [06 Motion](06-Motion.md), [07 Accessibility](07-Accessibility.md), and [08 Performance](08-Performance.md) to all visual decisions.

Every pixel should answer: “Does this increase trust?” If not, it should not exist.
